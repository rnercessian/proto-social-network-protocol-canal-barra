# Canal Barra Governance Phases

This document defines the governance phase terminology used in the Canal Barra digital archaeology repository.

It exists to avoid a misleading simplification: Canal Barra governance should not be described as a single-person "BarMan administration".

BarMan held founder status and played a central role in the creation and early institutional identity of #barra, but the channel's governance was collegial. It involved Masters, Operators, access lists, public rules, topic discipline, ChanServ configuration, operator meetings and website-based accountability.

## Phase 1: Founder Collegial Governance

**Founder Collegial Governance** refers to the earlier governance model in which BarMan's founder status coexisted with a collegial system of Masters, Operators, public rules and documented access procedures.

This phase should not be described as solitary founder rule.

The founder role mattered because it anchored channel continuity, access configuration and symbolic authority. But practical governance was distributed through a system of trusted participants and documented rules.

Core characteristics:

- Founder status existed as a technical and symbolic anchor.
- Masters and Operators participated in governance.
- Access decisions were not supposed to be improvised in the live channel.
- Operator legitimacy depended on trust, public responsibility and moral restraint.
- CanalBarra.com preserved rules, operator procedures and community-facing accountability.

## Phase 2: Master-led Operational Governance

**Master-led Operational Governance** refers to the later phase in which day-to-day operational authority shifted more clearly to the Masters.

This phase should not be described as beginning automatically on the day of the accident that cost Master operator Mad his life. The transition happened later, approximately two months after the accident, motivated by Mad's absence and by the founder's decision to step back from the channel's administrative life.

The founder did not disappear from Canal Barra as a social presence. He continued to attend some events promoted by the channel. The change was primarily administrative: the founder stepped away from routine governance and especially from administrative responsibility, while the channel continued under Master-led operational control.

Core characteristics:

- The absence of Master operator Mad created a governance and emotional rupture.
- The founder made a later decision to reduce administrative involvement.
- Masters became more visibly responsible for day-to-day operation.
- Inherited ChanServ configuration and public rules continued to shape the channel.
- The founder could still appear socially at Canal Barra events without remaining the central administrative actor.

## Terminology rule

Use:

```text
Founder Collegial Governance
```

for the earlier collegial model anchored by the founder role.

Use:

```text
Master-led Operational Governance
```

for the later operational model led more visibly by Masters after the founder's administrative withdrawal.

Avoid:

```text
BarMan administration
```

because it over-personalizes a structure that was technically, socially and institutionally collective.

## Evidence boundary

This terminology is interpretive and should be tied to dated evidence whenever possible.

The repository may use founder recollection to identify the transition pattern, but claims about exact dates, specific access-list changes or individual responsibility for later operator grants require dated logs, archived pages, participant statements or structured dataset records.
