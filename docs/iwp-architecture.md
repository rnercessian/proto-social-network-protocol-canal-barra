# The IRC-Web-Presence (IWP) Sociotechnical Pattern

## 1. Abstract and Scope

This document defines IRC-Web-Presence (IWP) as a descriptive sociotechnical pattern for analyzing pre-platform online communities that combined live channel-based interaction, web-backed persistence, nickname-level identity, public presence dynamics and local access governance.

IWP is not a new network protocol, not a replacement for IRC, and not a claim that all early online communities followed the same structure. It is an analytical pattern derived from material evidence of communities that used existing internet layers together: IRC for synchronous presence, the Web for persistence and memory, recurring public interaction for reputation, and local governance mechanisms for access control and order.

The Canal Barra / #barra community on BRASnet, founded in Rio de Janeiro in 1996, is treated in this repository as a documented Brazilian case study of the IWP pattern.

## 2. Why the Term Exists

Most platform-centered histories of social networking move from Classmates.com in 1995 to SixDegrees.com in 1997. This repository does not dispute those platform milestones. Instead, it introduces a documented Brazilian 1996 case that belongs to a different architectural lineage: an IRC-Web-Presence proto-social network rather than a centralized profile-graph platform.

The term IWP is an acronym for a material convergence observed in historical evidence:

- **IRC**: synchronous channel-based interaction and live public presence;
- **Web**: persistent publication, memory, profiles, rules, photographs, event records or community metadata;
- **Presence**: the public visibility of nicknames, status, reputation, timing, entrance, absence, performance and recognition inside the live social field.

IWP should be read as a descriptive typology, not as an invented protocol or a claim of formal standardization.

## 3. Definitional Boundary: What IWP Is and Is Not

### IWP is

- an analytical pattern for digital archaeology;
- a way to classify pre-platform online communities built from existing internet layers;
- a model for studying live presence, nickname-level identity, web-backed persistence and local governance;
- a lens for comparing channel-based communities that produced durable social identity without centralized profile-graph architecture.

### IWP is not

- a new internet protocol;
- a replacement or extension of RFC 1459;
- a claim that IRC itself was a social network in every use case;
- a claim that every IRC channel with a website qualifies as IWP;
- a corporate platform model;
- a profile-graph system equivalent to SixDegrees, Friendster or Facebook;
- a legal certification of historical priority.

## 4. Minimum Criteria for IWP Classification

A historical community should meet several material criteria before being classified as an IWP case. These criteria prevent the concept from becoming too broad.

### 4.1 Synchronous Presence Layer

The community must rely on a live interaction engine, usually IRC or a comparable synchronous chat protocol, where real-time presence is socially meaningful.

Presence includes more than being technically online. It includes being seen entering, leaving, speaking, changing nickname, holding silence, receiving attention, being addressed, being teased, being recognized or being ignored.

### 4.2 Web-Backed Persistence Layer

The community must have a web-backed layer that preserves or publishes social memory beyond the live channel.

This may include profiles, cadastros, photographs, event records, rules, statistics, archives, topic explanations, operator lists, community pages, announcements or cultural artifacts.

The key distinction is simple:

> IRC made the community live; the Web made parts of it persistent.

### 4.3 Nickname-Level Identity

The community must rely on nickname-level identity as a primary social identifier.

This does not require every nickname to be permanently registered or legally verified. A nickname may be registered, recurring, socially recognized, temporarily modified, stylized, performative or situational.

The important point is that social recognition operates at the nickname layer before it operates at the civil-identity layer.

### 4.4 Public Reputation and Presence Dynamics

The community must produce visible public reputation through live interaction.

Examples may include public greeting, teasing, status deflation, flirtatious reputation, topic propagation, nickname mythmaking, conflict, rivalry, moderation, recognition, absence, return, silence, collective jokes or public requests.

These dynamics should be treated as sociotechnical outputs of the presence layer, not as gossip detached from infrastructure.

### 4.5 Community Access Governance

The community must show evidence of local governance or access control, whether formal or informal.

In IRC cases, this may include Founder, Master, Operator, Voice, ban lists, access lists, channel rules, operator meetings, moderation practices, public warnings, kicks, bans, topic control or reputation-based access decisions.

IWP governance should not be reduced to rigid hierarchy. In many cases, authority may be negotiated through a mixture of access level, seniority, argument, trust, participation, practical judgment and public legitimacy.

### 4.6 Optional but Strong: External or Offline Social Anchoring

Offline or external social anchoring strengthens an IWP classification but should not be treated as mandatory for every historical case.

In Canal Barra, offline anchoring was central: IRContros, friendships, school and university ties, neighborhood identity, physical meetings and social circulation reinforced the reputation created in the channel.

For other communities, external anchoring may appear through mailing lists, fan clubs, schools, LAN houses, universities, BBS networks, conventions, local scenes, gaming groups or regional communities.

## 5. Difference Between IWP and Profile-Graph Platforms

IWP should not be confused with profile-graph platforms.

A profile-graph platform centers the user profile and explicit relationship links. SixDegrees is historically important because it stored relational profiles and made connections visible inside a centralized platform.

An IWP system centers live presence and channel-level interaction. Reputation is produced by recurring participation, public visibility, nickname recognition, web-backed memory and community governance.

A simplified distinction:

| Dimension | Profile-Graph Platform | IWP Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core object | User profile | Live nickname presence |
| Social graph | Explicit stored links | Implied reputation and recurring interaction |
| Main arena | Platform interface | Live channel |
| Persistence | Built into platform database | Often distributed between Web pages, archives and community memory |
| Identity | Profile-centered | Nickname-level, performative and recurring |
| Governance | Platform terms and account control | Local channel access, operators and community norms |
| Social proof | Connections, profiles, lists | Visibility, response, reputation, recognition and offline anchoring |

## 6. Canal Barra as Case Study

Canal Barra is treated as a case study because its historical record suggests the convergence of the IWP components:

- live IRC presence on BRASnet;
- CanalBarra.com as web-backed persistence and memory;
- nickname-level identity and reputation;
- local access governance through IRC roles, operator practices and documented meetings;
- social anchoring beyond the screen through IRContros and real-world circuits;
- public presence dynamics visible in academic and archival evidence.

This does not mean that every historical claim about Canal Barra is automatically proven by the IWP model. The model organizes the evidence; it does not replace evidence.

## 7. Evidence Requirements

IWP classification should be evidence-first.

Acceptable evidence may include:

- archived websites;
- IRC logs;
- academic studies;
- primary internal documents;
- operator meeting records;
- screenshots;
- structured datasets;
- timestamped event records;
- preserved profiles or cadastros;
- participant testimony clearly labeled as testimony;
- founder memory clearly labeled as contextual memory.

The model must preserve the difference between documented evidence, archived evidence, academic evidence, participant testimony, founder memory, hypothesis and interpretation.

## 8. Handling Nickname Volatility

Nickname volatility is not a weakness of the IWP model. It is part of the model.

In IWP communities, nicknames can operate simultaneously as:

- identity labels;
- status messages;
- jokes;
- requests;
- emotional signals;
- topic triggers;
- reputation markers;
- temporary performances;
- signs of affiliation or rivalry.

A single civil person may use multiple nicknames. A nickname may change form. A stable nickname may become a public character. A temporary nickname may carry a message to the channel.

Therefore, IWP data modeling should avoid assuming a simple one-to-one relationship between nickname and civil identity.

## 9. Governance Boundary

IWP governance should be modeled as local access governance, not as total control over the underlying network.

In Canal Barra, the #barra channel operated on BRASnet. The Canal Barra community did not control BRASnet itself, IRC as a protocol, or ChanServ as a universal network service. The community operated as a social and governance layer over shared IRC infrastructure.

This distinction matters:

> BRASnet provided transport and network services; Canal Barra produced the local social system, memory, reputation and governance around #barra.

## 10. Sociotechnical Dynamics

Some evidence records in this repository describe behavioral phenomena such as public banter, flirtatious reputation amplification, public status deflation, nickname-driven meme propagation, romantic rivalry regulation and public requests through nickname changes.

These should not be treated as isolated gossip. They are included because they show how the presence layer produced social effects in real time.

However, these dynamics are secondary to the architectural pattern. They should support the IWP model, not replace it.

Avoid:

- new protocol;
- formal internet standard;
- RFC proposal;
- legal proof;
- definitive proof;
- historical reparation;
- historical justice;
- proto-Facebook;
- chat room as the primary category;
- decentralized social media in the modern platform sense;
- claims that every IRC channel with a website was IWP.

## 13. Short Definition

> IRC-Web-Presence (IWP) is a sociotechnical pattern for pre-platform online communities where live channel-based presence, web-backed persistence, nickname-level identity and local access governance operate together to produce durable social reputation without a centralized profile-graph platform.
