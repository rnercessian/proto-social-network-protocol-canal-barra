# Network Autonomy and Founder Continuity

This document clarifies a distinction that is central to the Canal Barra case study: the founder's reduced active presence in the IRC channel must not be confused with abandonment of the Canal Barra ecosystem.

## Key distinction

The historical record should distinguish between:

1. **Active IRC channel management** — daily presence, direct channel moderation and access-list involvement around #barra on BRASnet.
2. **Web and institutional continuity** — continued maintenance of CanalBarra.com, preservation of the public portal, persistence of the Canal Barra name and later expansion of the brand into a formal company identity.

A reduction or end of active IRC-layer management does not mean the founder abandoned Canal Barra.

## Network autonomy

Network autonomy means that the Canal Barra social system could continue producing presence, recognition, conflict, status and interaction even when the founder was not the daily center of IRC activity.

This is relevant because a proto-social network is not proven only by the founder's control. It is also evidenced by the system's ability to keep generating social life, visibility and reputation through its participants, website, events and governance mechanisms.

## Founder continuity

The founder's relationship with Canal Barra continued through the web and institutional layers.

The archive therefore avoids language such as:

- "the founder abandoned Canal Barra";
- "the project ended when the founder left IRC";
- "the social network depended exclusively on the founder's presence".

Preferred language:

- "the founder reduced or ended active IRC-layer management";
- "CanalBarra.com continued as a maintained web layer";
- "the Canal Barra name and archive continued beyond the founder's daily IRC presence";
- "the IRC social layer showed network autonomy while the web/institutional layer preserved continuity".

## Relationship to external evidence

Independent academic or archival sources may document phases in which Canal Barra continued operating socially without centering the founder as the main visible actor.

Such evidence should be interpreted carefully:

- as support for network autonomy;
- not as proof of founder abandonment;
- not as proof that the web layer was inactive;
- not as proof that the Canal Barra identity ended with IRC-channel withdrawal.

## Methodological rule

When describing later phases of Canal Barra, evidence records must specify which layer is being discussed:

- IRC live presence layer;
- web portal and persistence layer;
- offline reputation and social field;
- access governance layer;
- institutional or brand continuity layer.

This prevents the archive from collapsing the entire Canal Barra ecosystem into only the IRC channel.
