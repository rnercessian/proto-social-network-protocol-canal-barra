# Historiographical Precedents: AIM, IRC and Proto-Social Networks

## Purpose

This document explains why the history of social networks cannot be reduced to web platforms with profile pages and explicit friend lists.

The purpose is not to deny the historical importance of platform-based social networking sites such as SixDegrees. The purpose is to show that other systems — including AIM, IRC channels and Canal Barra — performed social-network functions before or outside the dominant platform model.

## The Platform Bias

A large part of social-network history is written from the perspective of web-based commercial platforms. In that framework, systems are usually recognized when they include:

- a profile page;
- an explicit list of contacts or friends;
- the ability to browse or traverse those connections;
- a bounded web service controlled by a platform owner.

This framework is useful, but incomplete. It describes a type of platform. It does not fully describe the lived function of social networking.

## AIM as a Proto-Social Network Precedent

AOL Instant Messenger (AIM) is frequently remembered as more than a messaging tool. Its social importance came from screen names, buddy lists, away messages, profiles and the habit of checking the presence and status of other people.

AIM matters here because it shows that a system can become a proto-social network through use, even when it was not originally designed as a full social networking site.

AIM proves that social-network history cannot be reduced to profile pages.

## Canal Barra as a Brazilian Organic Case

Canal Barra extends this logic in the Brazilian IRC/BRASnet context.

If AIM can be understood as a proto-social network because it created persistent screen names, presence, status expression and lightweight public identity, Canal Barra deserves analysis as an organic social network because it added:

- a public community channel (#barra);
- persistent nicknames recognized across time;
- operators and access levels;
- public reputation and status;
- dated IRContros and physical meetings;
- VIP lists and event-based recognition;
- territorial identity in Barra da Tijuca, Rio de Janeiro;
- academic, literary and archival documentation.

## Core Historiographical Claim

SixDegrees is important for the history of platform-based social networking sites.

AIM is important as a proto-social network precedent.

Canal Barra is important as a documented Brazilian case of organic, lived, geographically anchored social networking before platform social media became dominant.

## Short Formula

**SixDegrees explains the platform. AIM opens the proto-social category. Canal Barra documents the lived Brazilian case.**
