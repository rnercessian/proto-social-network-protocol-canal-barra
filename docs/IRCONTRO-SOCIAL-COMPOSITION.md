# IRContro Social Composition

## Purpose

This document defines how the repository should describe the social composition of Canal Barra IRContros without reducing participants to gender, appearance or anecdotal impressions.

The goal is to document the offline trust layer of the community.

## Core Observation

Founder observation: although the 2002 cadastro dataset is numerically male-heavy, documented and remembered IRContros from the active period often showed a more socially mixed offline presence, with recurring female participation.

This should be treated as a community-composition observation, not as a statement about attractiveness, desirability or gender hierarchy.

## Why This Matters

The relevant historical point is not that women were present as decoration.

The relevant point is that Canal Barra generated enough trust, recognition and social safety for online interaction to become repeated physical community presence among a mixed group of participants.

This helps separate Canal Barra from the stereotype of a male-only technical chat space.

## Safe Analytical Framing

Use:

```text
socially mixed offline participation
recurring female participation
trust-based offline conversion
community legitimacy beyond the registration dataset
```

Avoid:

```text
women as proof of success
appearance-based descriptions
ranking participants by attractiveness
individual gender inference from images
sexist or competitive language
```

## Data Interpretation

The cadastro dataset and the IRContro layer should be read differently:

```text
cadastro dataset -> reach, registration, nickname persistence
IRContro evidence -> trust, physical presence, social conversion
```

A male-heavy registration base does not automatically mean a male-only community in practice.

A socially mixed IRContro record suggests that the offline layer of Canal Barra had broader social legitimacy than the raw registration ratio alone would imply.

## Evidence Requirements

Before making strong claims, the repository should prefer:

```text
captioned IRContro pages
photo manifests
participant testimony
founder statement clearly labeled
aggregate counts where consent-safe
no person-level gender tagging unless necessary and consent-aware
```

## Age-Related Boundary

Some IRContros included teenagers and young adults.

Do not infer individual ages from photographs.

Do not publish person-level age guesses.

If needed, use only broad, testimony-based wording:

```text
Founder memory: around 1999, many participants in photographed IRContros were teenagers and young adults.
```

## Core Sentence

**The important evidence is not gender as spectacle; it is Canal Barra's ability to convert online nicknames into repeated, socially mixed, trust-based physical community presence.**
