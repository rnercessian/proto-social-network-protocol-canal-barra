# Canal Barra 5th Anniversary Party — Music Evidence

## Separate predecessor events — 15 and 29 October 2000

The **Banda do Canal Barra performed two festival appearances** associated with the Clube Carioca, documented retrospectively and prospectively on an archived 2001 website announcement. They are **two distinct musical IRContros** indexed at the same chronological level as this 2002 fifth-anniversary celebration, **not this same event**. See [the separate Clube Carioca dossier](CLUBE-CARIOCA-MUSICAL-IRCONTROS-2000.md) and [the IRContros chronological index](../../docs/DOCUMENTED-IRCONTROS-CHRONOLOGY.md). The eight original compositions announced for October 2000 are not to be conflated with the founder-provided 15-item 2002 anniversary setlist.

## Source Context

Founder statement: the Canal Barra 5th anniversary party took place in 2002 at Ilha dos Pescadores.

The event included live music and multiple bands.

According to the founder:

- **Malucos da Ponte** performed at the party;
- the band had originally been assembled through the announcement/classified layer of the Canal Barra website under the name **Banda do Canal Barra**;
- the band was renamed **Malucos da Ponte** in 2000;
- four other bands also performed;
- one of the other bands was **MHz**;
- the participants were connected, or in a testing period, with Serafim from **TecoTeco Produções**.

## Private Confirmation — MHz

A private 2025 message exchange with Luiz Olivetti confirmed that the band name was **MHz**.

The raw private conversation should not be published in this repository without permission.

For public archival purposes, this repository only preserves the historical fact relevant to the event:

> MHz was one of the bands associated with the Canal Barra 5th anniversary party in 2002.

Personal comments and private details from the conversation are excluded.

## Why This Matters

The 5th anniversary party is important because it shows that Canal Barra was not only a digital community.

It produced:

- physical events;
- live music;
- cultural collaboration;
- band formation;
- event programming;
- connections with local music/business structures.

This reinforces the broader thesis that Canal Barra functioned as a lived social network: it moved from screen to website, from website to event, and from event to cultural production.

## Link to the Website Layer

The early Canal Barra website included announcements. According to the founder, the Banda do Canal Barra was assembled through one of those announcements.

This means the website functioned as a social coordination layer, not merely as a static page.

## Link to Mad

Mad appears in the 1999 access-list material. Founder statement connects Mad to the later Banda do Canal Barra / Malucos da Ponte cultural layer.
